# Licensing Sijil

Sijil is **source-available**. The code is public, and you can read, run, modify and evaluate it. Production use needs a commercial license, except for the free uses listed below.

| Part | License | Commercial use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Everything except `verifier/` (proxy, policy engine, record store, report, console, scripts) | [Business Source License 1.1](LICENSE) | Free for the uses below. Other production use needs a commercial license |
| `verifier/` (the independent chain verifier) | [Apache License 2.0](verifier/LICENSE) | Free, including commercial use and redistribution |

On **2029-09-25** this version of Sijil converts to the Apache License 2.0 in full.

## Free, no license needed

- Evaluating, testing and developing with Sijil.
- Research, education, and personal non-commercial use.
- **One pilot deployment per organisation, for up to 60 days**, including in production.
- Running, modifying and redistributing the verifier for any purpose. Auditors, regulators and integrators can check a Sijil chain with no agreement with us.

## Needs a commercial license

- Running Sijil in production beyond the 60-day pilot.
- Integrators and platform vendors including Sijil in a paid delivery, bid, or managed service for a customer.
- Offering Sijil, or a modified version of it, to third parties as part of a product or hosted service.

Commercial licenses are priced per deployed model per year. They include the right to run Sijil in production for the licensed deployments, updates, and deployment support.

## How to get one

Email **shameer@infinitebarakah.com** with the organisation, the environment (on-premise, private cloud, or mixed), and roughly how many models Sijil will sit in front of.

Integrators bidding government work: tell us about the opportunity. We'll write the technical response section for it, at no cost, before any commercial agreement.

## Why the verifier is Apache-2.0

Sijil's core claim is that the thing that checks the records isn't the thing that wrote them. Anyone should be able to inspect, run and redistribute the verifier without asking us, so it's fully open source.
